The problem, in dollars

Airlines lose money in three places

Storms

$347k

what an average storm day costs when the airline simply waits it out

With the twin: $251k. A saving of $97k.

Delays

$418k

a day in routine delay costs: about 59 late flights, 72 minutes each, at $98.41 a minute

Ranks the riskiest flights 2 hours ahead.

Fuel

−$300k

a day at October 6 jet fuel of $4.34 a gallon, with fares unchanged. 42 of 49 routes lose money

Fares must rise 14.4% to earn 6% again.

Show, not tell

The product is live, right here

  1. Fuel. Set the October 6 fuel price and watch the routes that lose money turn red.
  2. Risk. A model tested on 612,038 real July 2026 flights ranks every departure two hours ahead.
  3. Storm. Close O'Hare for four hours. The twin tests thousands of recoveries and shows the trade between money and passengers.
  4. Ask. Claude explains the plan and drafts the passenger notice. It cannot approve a plan. A person does.

The ask

One hub. 26 weeks.
A saving finance can sign off.

DiscoverWeeks 1 to 3
ConnectWeeks 4 to 9, the client's operations, crew and cost data
ModelWeeks 8 to 15, rebuild the twin and retrain on their flights
ShadowWeeks 16 to 21, run beside real controllers
AssistWeeks 22 to 26, a person approves every plan

The same pattern then carries to other sectors: simulate the operation, find the best response, let AI explain it, keep a person in charge.
